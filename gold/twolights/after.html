Two lights, & twenty miles

A walk along the coast — from Portland Bill to Start Point — and what the beams taught me about patience. Both towers were lit before my grandparents were born. Neither one cares whether I arrived.

The walks

Sunrise · 6:40 AM

Portland Bill

Isle of Portland, Dorset · lit 1906

A 41-metre tower painted red and white, standing on the Isle of Portland since 1906. The beam reaches 25 nautical miles. We arrived at 6:40 am. The sea rolled in heavy, slate-grey and cream — the same two colours as the tower.

Sunset · Hallsands Bay

Start Point

Devon headland · lit 1836

A 28-metre granite tower on a jagged headland, lit in 1836. The beam sweeps every 2.7 seconds. We caught it from Hallsands, across the bay — two flashes, eleven seconds of dark, two flashes again.

What I learned

  • 01 · Lesson

    Rhythm is older than urgency

    Both lights have been flashing since before my grandparents were born. Neither tower cares whether I arrived.

  • 02 · Lesson

    Twenty miles is a small country

    The two towers are a morning's walk apart — but their beams never cross. They take turns with the horizon.

  • 03 · Lesson

    Cold air carries further

    The clearest view came when the temperature dropped. The beam sharpened. The fog lifted. The lesson wrote itself.
