What can we count without inventing a number?

Six dated things. One magnitude.

The notebook names three places and three signals, each with a year. Position on a shared 1836–1995 scale is the encoding. The only area it ever gives — Big Bend’s eight hundred thousand acres — is drawn as eight equal units, not a bigger park.

04 · Places — small multiples

1836 · San Antonio

Alamo

A small stone mission at the center of the Texas Republic. Nineteen days, one long siege, one story that carried a state.

1944 · west Texas

Big Bend

Eight hundred thousand acres along the Rio Grande. Cottonwood, prickly pear, and one of the darkest night skies in North America.

1839 · on the Colorado

Austin

Live music, live oaks, live legislature. Named after Stephen F. Austin, the empresario who first brought settlers under Mexican rule.

Schematic pins — not a survey

05 · Signals

1839 · one five-point star

Lone Star flag

Blue for loyalty, white for purity, red for bravery. The only US state flag that once flew over a sovereign republic.

1901 · blooms in spring

Bluebonnet

State flower. Only along the highway shoulders and the pastures where the ranchers let it.

1995 · seven feet tip to tip

Longhorn

State large mammal. A living remnant of the Spanish criollo cattle. Length, not area: the horns as a measure.

Finding: six dated names sit on one year scale. The notebook invents no other statistic. The acres of Big Bend are the only magnitude, so they are counted as units, not drawn as a bigger Texas.
